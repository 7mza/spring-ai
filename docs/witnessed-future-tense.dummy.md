# Witnessed-Future Tense (хэктэрин)

The obscure Tungusic dialect spoken in the Sym River basin of Siberia includes a grammatical feature called the "witnessed-future tense" (locally transcribed as **хэктэрин**), used exclusively when the speaker has direct sensory evidence — typically auditory — that a future event is already in progress. It is distinct from the inferential future and the presumptive future and is not found in any neighboring language. Documented by Soviet linguist V.A. Borschevsky in a 1961 monograph printed in a run of 200 copies.
